# ICLR Review Form (observed verbatim from ICLR 2026 official reviews)

Every review MUST contain exactly these fields, in this order. Field names and
scale wordings below were extracted from real ICLR 2026 reviews and must be
reproduced exactly.

## Fields

1. **Summary:** — a neutral 1–2 paragraph restatement of what the paper does,
   claims, and shows. No evaluation here. Reviewers write this to prove they
   read the paper; authors and ACs use it to detect misreadings.
2. **Soundness:** — 1–4 scale: `1: poor`, `2: fair`, `3: good`, `4: excellent`
3. **Presentation:** — same 1–4 scale
4. **Contribution:** — same 1–4 scale
5. **Strengths:** — bulleted or numbered; specific, grounded in the manuscript
6. **Weaknesses:** — bulleted or numbered; the substantive core of the review
7. **Questions:** — questions the authors can answer in a rebuttal.
   `Please see weaknesses.` is acceptable and common when the weaknesses
   already end in implicit questions.
8. **Flag For Ethics Review:** — normally `No ethics review needed.`
9. **Rating:** — 10-point scale; observed labels:
   - `2: reject, not good enough`
   - `4: marginally below the acceptance threshold. But would not mind if paper is accepted`
   - `6: marginally above the acceptance threshold. But would not mind if paper is rejected`
   - `8: accept, good paper (poster)`
   - `10: strong accept, should be highlighted at the conference`
   Odd ratings (1/3/5/7/9) are essentially never used; pick from {2, 4, 6, 8, 10}.
10. **Confidence:** — 1–5 scale; observed labels:
    - `3: You are fairly confident in your assessment. It is possible that you did not understand some parts of the submission or that you are unfamiliar with some pieces of related work.`
    - `4: You are confident in your assessment, but not absolutely certain. It is unlikely, but not impossible, that you did not understand some parts of the submission or that you are unfamiliar with some pieces of related work.`
    Confidence 3 and 4 dominate; 5 is rare, 2 signals a reviewer outside their area.
11. **Code Of Conduct:** `Yes`

## Style norms observed in real reviews

- Weaknesses are usually **numbered (W1/W2/… or 1./2./…)**, each with a bold
  or one-line title followed by concrete argumentation. 3–6 numbered
  weaknesses is the norm for a below-threshold review; a positive review may
  have as few as 1–2.
- Every substantive claim points at something concrete: a table, figure,
  equation, section number, line number, or a **named prior paper**. Reviews
  that criticize without pointing at anything are treated as weak reviews.
- Harshness varies by reviewer, not by outcome: accepted papers still receive
  ratings of 2 with sarcastic prose. Tone ranges from collegial and hedged
  ("I doubt this will be a real issue") to adversarial first-person prose.
- Length correlates with rating direction: the most negative reviewer is
  usually the longest and most systematic; the most positive is often the
  shortest.
- Strengths are genuine, not throat-clearing — even harsh reviews concede
  real strengths ("the trilemma framing is reasonable, I guess").
- Questions are answerable-in-rebuttal asks: missing baselines, clarification
  of setup, requested ablations — not rhetorical attacks.
